MEMBER RESOURCES

Know the role.
Own the skill.

Every meeting assignment is practice for something larger than Toastmasters.

ROLE LAB

Meeting roles are
leadership reps.

Use these guides before a meeting, then look past the lectern. The same skills show up in boardrooms, interviews, negotiations, client meetings, team leadership and public life.

01
CHAIRMAN

Run the room with order.

+

What you are responsible for

Preside over the meeting, maintain order, move the agenda forward, recognize speakers and protect the meeting's structure.

Sample script

“Good evening, gentlemen. The meeting of the First Bahamas Branch of Toastmasters Club 1600 will now come to order. Our agenda has been circulated. We will proceed with the business of the evening.”

Real-life application

Chairing boards, committee meetings, AGMs, stakeholder sessions and any environment where authority has to feel calm rather than loud.

02
TOASTMASTER OF THE EVENING

Host the experience.

+

What you are responsible for

Set the tone, introduce the meeting theme, connect programme segments and keep energy consistent from opening to close.

Sample script

“Tonight's theme is ‘___’. By the end of this meeting, I want us to leave with one idea we can actually use. To begin our programme, allow me to introduce…”

Real-life application

Event hosting, emceeing, moderating conferences, client presentations and leading a meeting without making yourself the meeting.

03
TABLE TOPICS MASTER

Create pressure on purpose.

+

What you are responsible for

Design concise prompts, call participants fairly, manage pacing and create an environment where members can practise thinking on their feet.

Sample script

“For Table Topics tonight, each speaker will have one to two minutes. Listen carefully to the question, decide your position quickly, then give us a beginning, middle and end.”

Real-life application

Interviews, media questions, negotiations, impromptu remarks, sales calls and those beautiful moments when somebody says, “Amar, say a few words.”

04
SPEECH EVALUATOR

Turn observation into value.

+

What you are responsible for

Identify what worked, explain why it worked and give specific, usable recommendations tied to the speaker's objective.

Sample script

“Your strongest choice tonight was __ because __. One area that could make the message land harder is __. On your next delivery, I would test __.”

Real-life application

Performance reviews, coaching, creative direction, management and giving feedback without turning it into either fluff or a public execution.

05
GENERAL EVALUATOR

See the whole system.

+

What you are responsible for

Evaluate the meeting itself: execution, role performance, transitions, timing, standards and the quality of the overall member experience.

Sample script

“I will evaluate tonight through three lenses: structure, execution and experience. What strengthened the meeting was __. What reduced its effectiveness was __. The adjustment I recommend is __.”

Real-life application

Operations reviews, post-mortems, project management, quality assurance and learning to diagnose systems rather than blaming individuals.

06
LEXICOLOGIST / GRAMMARIAN

Make language intentional.

+

What you are responsible for

Introduce the word of the evening, listen for strong language and note grammar, phrasing and word choice worth highlighting.

Sample script

“Our word of the evening is ‘___’, meaning ___. Listen for opportunities to use it naturally. I will also note particularly effective language and any recurring grammatical issues.”

Real-life application

Writing, negotiations, executive communication, sales, brand messaging and becoming the person who notices when one word changes the entire meaning.

07
TIMER

Respect the clock.

+

What you are responsible for

Track speaking times accurately, signal speakers according to the assigned limits and report results at the appropriate point in the meeting.

Sample script

“I will time each speaker according to the programme. Green indicates the minimum time, yellow the target range and red the maximum. I will report qualifying times when called upon.”

Real-life application

Pitches, presentations, meetings, interviews and learning the brutal professional truth that a good point delivered three minutes late is often a bad point.

08
SERGEANT-AT-ARMS

Engineer the environment.

+

What you are responsible for

Prepare the room, protect meeting protocol, manage physical logistics, support guests and ensure the environment is ready before the programme begins.

Sample script

“Gentlemen and guests, please take your seats as we prepare to begin. Kindly place mobile devices on silent. At this time, I invite everyone to rise as we formally open tonight's meeting.”

Real-life application

Event operations, hospitality, logistics, protocol and understanding that leadership often starts before anyone notices you working.

09
PREPARED SPEAKER

Build an idea worth remembering.

+

What you are responsible for

Deliver a speech with a clear objective, intentional structure and choices that connect with the audience.

Simple structure

Hook → central idea → proof/story → meaning → call to action. Your speech should know where it is going before the audience has to figure it out for you.

Real-life application

Pitches, keynotes, board presentations, sermons, sales conversations, ceremonies and any moment when your idea has to survive outside your own head.

USE THE ROOM

Then take the role.

The skill only becomes yours after repetition.
